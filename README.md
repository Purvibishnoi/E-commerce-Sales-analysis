# 📊 Ecommerce Sales Analysis

## 🧾 Overview
This project analyzes e-commerce sales data using Microsoft Excel to evaluate business performance across revenue, profit, product categories, and customer segments.

The analysis focuses on identifying growth trends, profitability gaps, and structural risks within the business using real transactional data.

---

## 🎯 Objective
- Analyze sales and profit performance over time  
- Evaluate category and sub-category contributions  
- Identify profitability issues  
- Understand regional and segment-wise trends  
- Generate actionable business insights  

---

## 📁 Dataset Description
The dataset contains transactional-level e-commerce data with the following fields:

- Order Date (Year/Month)  
- Sales Revenue  
- Profit  
- Quantity Sold  
- Discount  
- Product Category & Sub-Category  
- Customer Segment  
- Region & State  

**Time Period:** 2011 – 2014  

---

## 🧹 Data Preparation
The dataset was cleaned and structured before analysis:

- Removed duplicate entries  
- Standardized date formats for time analysis  
- Verified numerical consistency (Sales, Profit, Quantity)  
- Ensured proper categorization of products and regions  

---

## 📊 Analysis Summary

### Overall Performance
- **Total Sales:** ~$2.29M  
- **Total Profit:** ~$286K  
- Business shows consistent growth over the years  

### Yearly Breakdown

| Year | Sales | Profit | Profit Margin |
|------|------|--------|--------------|
| 2011 | $484K | $49K | ~10.2% |
| 2012 | $470K | $61K | ~13.1% |
| 2013 | $608K | $81K | ~13.4% |
| 2014 | $733K | $93K | ~12.7% |

### Key Observations
- Strong growth from 2012 → 2014  
- Profit increases each year, but not proportionally to sales  
- Profit margin improves initially but slightly declines in 2014  

---

## 📈 Key Insights

### 1. Growth is Real but Not Efficient
Sales increased significantly from 2011 to 2014, but profit margins remain relatively low (~12–13%).  
➡️ The business is scaling revenue faster than profitability.

### 2. Discounts Are Hurting Profit
Presence of high discounts in the dataset directly impacts profit (including negative profit in some cases).  
➡️ Some sales are effectively unprofitable.

### 3. Category Performance Imbalance
- Technology and Office Supplies drive most revenue  
- Furniture frequently shows weaker profitability  

➡️ Not all categories contribute equally to business success.

### 4. Revenue Concentration
A small number of subcategories contribute a large share of total sales.  
➡️ High dependency on limited products increases risk.

### 5. Seasonal Sales Pattern
Sales tend to increase toward year-end (Q4).  
➡️ Business relies heavily on seasonal demand cycles.

---

## 💡 Recommendations

### Improve Profit Margins
- Reduce excessive discounting  
- Identify and fix loss-making products  
- Optimize pricing strategy  

### Control Discount Strategy
- Avoid deep discounts on already low-margin items  
- Use targeted discounts instead of blanket reductions  

### Optimize Product Mix
- Focus on high-margin products  
- Reassess underperforming categories like Furniture  

### Reduce Dependency Risk
- Expand and promote mid-performing products  
- Avoid over-reliance on a few top-selling subcategories  

### Leverage Seasonality
- Increase inventory and marketing before peak periods  
- Align promotions with demand cycles

## ⚠️ Limitations
- No detailed cost structure beyond profit  
- Customer behavior (retention, lifetime value) not included  
- External factors (competition, market trends) not considered  

---

## 🧠 Conclusion
The business demonstrates strong revenue growth over time, but profitability does not scale at the same rate.  

The key issue is not lack of sales—but **inefficient sales driven by discounting, category imbalance, and product dependency**. Addressing these areas is critical for sustainable growth.
